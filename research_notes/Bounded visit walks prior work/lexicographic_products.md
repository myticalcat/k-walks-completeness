# Hamiltonicity of lexicographic products G[K̄_k] (blow-ups by independent sets): characterizations and complexity

Notation used below: G[K̄_k] = lexicographic product / composition of G with the edgeless graph on k vertices (also written G∘K̄_k, G∘N_k, G[E_k]; for k = 2 also called the "double graph" D[G]). In the k-walk literature: a **k-walk** is a spanning closed walk visiting every vertex at most k times; an **exact k-walk** is a spanning closed walk visiting every vertex exactly k times (Jackson–Wormald define both as connected spanning sub-multigraphs of (2k)×G with all degrees even and ≤ 2k, resp. exactly 2k). In Liu–Sheffield–Westover's notation, "exact k-walk" = "[k,k]-HAM".

Note on search hygiene: a GitHub repo "myticalcat/k-walks-completeness" surfaced in searches stating "EXACT-2 on G is the same problem as HAMILTONIAN PATH on G[K̄₂]" — this appears to be the user's own repo, not prior work, and is excluded from the findings below.

## Key Question 1: Is the walk/visit-count ⇔ Hamiltonicity-of-G[K̄_k] equivalence already stated in the literature?

### Takeaway
Yes, for the **closed** version it is explicitly stated in 1990: Jackson & Wormald write that, for k ≥ 2, G has an exact k-walk (closed spanning walk visiting each vertex exactly k times) iff G[K̄_k] has a Hamilton cycle, and G has a k-walk (≤ k visits) iff G[K_k] is Hamiltonian. The **open-walk / Hamiltonian-path** version (walk visiting each vertex exactly k times ⇔ G[K̄_k] traceable) was **not found stated explicitly** anywhere; it is the same trivial labelling argument.

### Cited Findings
- **Explicitly stated (closed walks).** Jackson & Wormald, "k-walks of graphs", *Australasian Journal of Combinatorics* 2 (1990) 135–146 (Zbl 0757.05074), §1 Introduction, verbatim (OCR of the journal scan; the bar over K is lost in OCR, see Inferences): "Given two graphs G and H, the composition of G and H, denoted by G[H], is defined as the graph with vertex set V(G)×V(H) and edge set {(u1,v1)(u2,v2): u1u2 ∈ E(G) or u1 = u2 and v1v2 ∈ E(H)}. Note that for a graph with at least three vertices, every 1-walk is a Hamilton cycle. On the other hand, for k ≥ 2, G has a k-walk (or exact k-walk) if and only if G[K_k] (or G[K̄_k], respectively) has a Hamilton cycle. Thus we may use results on Hamilton cycles to obtain results on k-walks. There is a strong relationship between k-walks and the hamiltonicity of compositions, since if H has a Hamilton path then G[H] is hamiltonian if and only if G has a |V(H)|-walk. We exploit a similar connection in examining the complexity of finding k-walks (see Section 6)." — [Jackson & Wormald 1990 (PDF)](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/2/ocr-ajc-v2-p135.pdf)
- Jackson & Wormald's formal definition: "An exact k-walk (or k-walk) of G is a connected spanning subgraph W of (2k)×G, such that the degree of each vertex v in W is 2k (or is an even number which is at most 2k, respectively)… Euler's Theorem implies that a k-walk possesses a closed walk traversing each edge exactly once (an Euler tour), and so a graph with a k-walk (or exact k-walk) possesses a closed walk passing through each vertex at most k times (or exactly k times, respectively)." — [Jackson & Wormald 1990](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/2/ocr-ajc-v2-p135.pdf)
- Jackson & Wormald credit the "exactly k times" concept to Broersma: "Broersma [B2] considered closed walks visiting each vertex of a graph exactly k times", where [B2] = "H. Broersma, k-traceable graphs (submitted to Discrete Math.)". — [Jackson & Wormald 1990](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/2/ocr-ajc-v2-p135.pdf)
- That Broersma preprint appears to have been published as H. J. Broersma & F. Göbel, "k-traversable graphs", *Ars Combinatoria* 29A (1990) 141–153, Zbl 0722.05045 (MSC 05C45, 05C38, 05C78; zbMATH review text unavailable due to licensing). Liu–Sheffield–Westover cite [BG90] as the origin of "for fixed k, is there a closed walk that visits each vertex exactly k times, or at least once and at most k times". — [zbMATH entry](https://zbmath.org/4189769); [Ars Combin. vol. 29A](https://combinatorialpress.com/ars/vol29a/); [Liu, Sheffield, Westover 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16270)
- The same paper also uses the equivalence in the other direction for D_λ-cycles: "Clearly, G[K_k] has a D_k-cycle if and only if G has a k-walk" (§5), and applies Hamiltonicity theorems (Veldman, Fraisse, Chvátal–Erdős, Fleischner) to G[K_k] to derive k-walk results (Thms 5.1–5.4). — [Jackson & Wormald 1990](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/2/ocr-ajc-v2-p135.pdf)
- Liu, Sheffield & Westover (arXiv:2405.16270, May 2024; no journal version found) state the multigraph/Euler-tour form of the equivalence (not the lexicographic form): "G is [a,b]-HAM if and only if 2b·G contains a spanning even [2a,2b] factor; that is, a connected subgraph containing all vertices of G, with every degree even and in [2a,2b]. The equivalence of this formulation follows from taking an Euler tour." They also give the edge-labelling form (labels = traversal counts, label sums at each vertex even in [2a,2b], support connected). — [arXiv:2405.16270](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16270)
- Kriesell (1997) gives, for general H, a "connected 2|H|-regular multiple" sufficient condition (see Key Question 2), which for H = K̄_k is exactly "G has a connected loopless 2k-regular multigraph on its edges", i.e. an exact k-walk. — [Ekstein & Teska, restating Kriesell's Theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.10123)
- Terminology: the "double graph" D[G] of Munarini et al. (Discrete Math. 2008) is defined as G × T₂ (T₂ = K₂ with loops), equivalently the lexicographic product G∘N₂ with N₂ the edgeless graph on two vertices, i.e. D[G] = G[K̄_2]. — [Wikipedia: Double graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_graph)

### Inferences
- The OCR line reads "G[K;J (or G[K k], respectively)"; given the parenthetical pairing "(or exact k-walk) … (or …, respectively)" and the mathematics (consecutive copies of v can only be traversed in one visit if the copies are adjacent), the first is G[K_k] (k-walk) and the second must be G[K̄_k] (exact k-walk). Anyone quoting this should check the typeset original; the logic leaves no doubt.
- The open-walk version (walk visiting every vertex exactly k times ⇔ Hamiltonian path in G[K̄_k], via "label the i-th visit of v with the i-th copy of v") is the obvious analogue, but I found no paper stating it. Broersma's preprint title "k-traceable graphs" hints that open walks might have been considered, but the published title is "k-traversable graphs", and both secondary sources describe only closed walks.
- "Double graph" is a useful extra search term for k = 2. I found no Hamiltonicity characterization for double graphs; see Gaps.

### Gaps
- Full text of Broersma & Göbel (Ars Combin. 29A, 1990) could not be accessed, and zbMATH's review is withheld. So I could not confirm whether they (a) mention lexicographic products, (b) treat open walks or "k-traceability", or (c) use exactly the pendant-triangle gadget.
- Teichert (1983) and the Schaar–Sonntag–Teichert monograph (1988) were not accessible in full. I could not confirm whether they state the K̄_k walk equivalence.
- The Hammack–Imrich–Klavžar *Handbook of Product Graphs* chapter on Hamiltonicity was not checked (no accessible full text found).

## Key Question 2: What structural characterizations or conditions are known for G[K̄_k] being Hamiltonian or traceable?

### Takeaway
The only exact characterization is the trivial one: G[K̄_k] is Hamiltonian iff G has an exact k-walk, iff G has a connected spanning loopless 2k-regular multigraph on E(G). Beyond that, the literature has necessary conditions (a toughness-type inequality), monotonicity in k, sufficient conditions (claw-freeness plus minimum degree, squares, Dirac, cubic bridgeless), and a complete answer only when G is a path. No toughness or f-factor characterization was found.

### Cited Findings
**Exact characterizations**
- Jackson–Wormald (1990), §1: for k ≥ 2, G[K̄_k] has a Hamilton cycle iff G has an exact k-walk. — [JW90](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/2/ocr-ajc-v2-p135.pdf)
- Kriesell, "A note on Hamiltonian cycles in lexicographical products", *J. Autom. Lang. Comb.* 2(2) (1997) 135–138, Theorem (restated as Theorem 4 by Ekstein–Teska): "Let G and H be graphs. If G has a connected, 2|H|-regular multiple G′ satisfying π(H) ≥ ℓ_{G′}(x) for all x ∈ V(G′), then G[H] contains a hamiltonian cycle that contains exactly m_{G′}(xy) edges between [the copies of H at x and y]." Here π(H) is the maximum number of edges of a spanning linear forest of H, and ℓ is the number of loops. — [Ekstein & Teska arXiv:2012.10123](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.10123); [zbMATH Zbl 0883.68102](https://zbmath.org/1089088)
- Kriesell's zbMATH summary: "We present some sufficient conditions in terms of toughness and factors which are much weaker in many cases. A typical statement is that G[H] is hamiltonian if G is 2-edge connected and cubic and |H| ≥ 2." This covers H = K̄_k for every k ≥ 2. — [zbMATH](https://zbmath.org/1089088)
- Liu–Sheffield–Westover: [a,b]-HAM ⇔ there is an edge labelling with connected support whose label sums at each vertex are even and lie in [2a,2b]. For a = b = k this is the exact k-walk / G[K̄_k]-Hamiltonicity condition. — [arXiv:2405.16270](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16270)

**Necessary conditions**
- Jackson–Wormald Lemma 2.1(ii), attributed to Broersma [B2, Proposition 2.1]: "If G has an exact k-walk then c(G − S) + (k − 1)c₀(G − S) ≤ k|S| for all nonempty proper subsets S of V(G)." Here c₀ counts isolated vertices of G − S. Lemma 2.1(i): a k-walk implies c(G − S) ≤ k|S|, i.e. G is (1/k)-tough. — [JW90](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/2/ocr-ajc-v2-p135.pdf)
- JW Remark 2.1: the 1/k-toughness condition is not sufficient for k-walks. For any ε > 0 there are (1/k + 2/(3k²) − ε)-tough graphs with no k-walk. — [JW90](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/2/ocr-ajc-v2-p135.pdf)

**Monotonicity in k**
- JW: "One interesting result from [B2, Corollary 3.3] is that if a graph has an exact k-walk then it has an exact (k + 1)-walk (k ≥ 1)." So G[K̄_k] Hamiltonian ⇒ G[K̄_{k+1}] Hamiltonian. — [JW90](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/2/ocr-ajc-v2-p135.pdf)
- Liu–Sheffield–Westover Lemma 3 (attributed to [BG90]): "If a graph G is [b,b]-HAM for some b, then it's also [b′,b′]-HAM for all b′ > b." Proof: take a 2-factor of the 2b-regular multigraph (Petersen/Tutte 2-factor theorem) and double it. — [arXiv:2405.16270](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16270)

**Sufficient conditions for exact k-walks (Hamiltonicity of G[K̄_k])**
- JW Theorem 3.1(ii): if G is connected, K_{1,k+1}-free, and δ(G) ≥ k, then G has an exact k-walk. — [JW90](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/2/ocr-ajc-v2-p135.pdf)
- JW Theorem 5.6: "If G is connected and δ(G) ≥ 2 then G² has an exact 2-walk", i.e. G²[K̄_2] is Hamiltonian. — [JW90](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/2/ocr-ajc-v2-p135.pdf)
- JW Remark 5.1: for exact k-walks the best minimum-degree condition is |V(G)|/2 for every k. Dirac plus monotonicity gives sufficiency, and K_{m+1,m} shows sharpness. — [JW90](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/2/ocr-ajc-v2-p135.pdf)
- Kouider & Vestergaard (Discuss. Math. Graph Theory 24 (2004) 431–441), as summarized by Liu et al.: for a ≥ 2, any 2-connected graph with minimum degree an/(a+b) is [a,b]-HAM. For a = b = k this is δ ≥ n/2. — [arXiv:2405.16270](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16270)
- Liu et al. Lemma 2 + Lemma 3: every connected d-regular graph is [k,k]-HAM for k ≥ d/2 (d even, from an Euler tour) and for k ≥ d (d odd, from doubled edges). — [arXiv:2405.16270](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16270)

**Path base graphs (complete answer)**
- Ekstein & Teska, "Hamiltonian properties in generalized lexicographic products", *Discuss. Math. Graph Theory* 45(1) (2025) 219–237 (arXiv:2012.10123), Theorems 2–3: complete characterizations of when P_n[H] is Hamiltonian, traceable or Hamiltonian-connected, in terms of π(H). For H = K̄_k (π = 0): P_{2m}[K̄_k] is always traceable and is Hamiltonian only for m = 1; P_{2m+1}[K̄_k] is never Hamiltonian, and is traceable iff k ≤ 1. (This is my specialization of their theorems; P_2[K̄_k] = K_{k,k}.) They remark that when G has no Hamiltonian path, "it seems to be complicated to characterize when G[H] is traceable, hamiltonian or hamiltonian connected." — [arXiv:2012.10123](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.10123)
- Teichert, "Hamiltonian properties of the lexicographic product of undirected graphs", *Elektron. Informationsverarb. Kybernet.* 19 (1983) 67–77 (Zbl 0535.05039). zbMATH review: Theorem 1 says if each G_i is traceable with |V(G_i)| ≥ i+1, the product is strongly path-connected. The next two theorems give "(possibly sharp) sufficient conditions for G1·G2 to be traceable, homogeneously traceable, or Hamiltonian when either G1 or G2 is assumed traceable. Two measures of non-traceability are involved." — [zbMATH Zbl 0535.05039](https://zbmath.org/?q=an:0535.05039)
- Teichert / Kriesell (as restated by Ekstein–Teska, Theorem 5): if G has a Hamiltonian path and |G| is even, then G[H] is traceable (and Hamiltonian if H has an edge). If |G| is odd, (|H|−1)/2 ≤ π(H) gives traceability. For H = K̄_k only the even-order traceability statement applies. — [arXiv:2012.10123](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.10123)
- Schaar, Sonntag & Teichert, *Hamiltonian properties of products of graphs and digraphs*, Teubner-Texte zur Math. 108 (1988), is a survey monograph covering traceability, Hamiltonicity etc. for the lexicographic and other classical products. — [zbMATH Zbl 0678.05033](https://zbmath.org/?q=an:0678.05033)

**Other lexicographic-product Hamiltonicity results (not specific to K̄_k)**
- Baranyai & Szász, "Hamiltonian decomposition of lexicographic product", *JCTB* 31 (1981) 253–261 (Hamiltonian decompositions; G and H Hamiltonian-decomposable). — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0095895681900289)
- Kaiser & Kriesell, "On the pancyclicity of lexicographic products", *Graphs Combin.* 22 (2006) 51–58: G[H] is weakly pancyclic if G and H each have an edge, and if G is 4-tough and ||H|| ≥ 1 then G[H] is Hamiltonian. Both require H to have an edge, so neither covers K̄_k. — [as cited in Ekstein & Teska](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.10123)

### Inferences
- Exact k-walks are not monotone with the "at most k" hierarchy (Ham path ⇒ prism-Hamiltonian ⇒ 2-walk ⇒ 3-tree ⇒ 3-walk …). A Hamiltonian path does not give an exact 2-walk: P_n[K̄_2] is non-Hamiltonian for n ≥ 3 (Ekstein–Teska). More generally, any G ≠ K_2 with a degree-1 vertex has no exact k-walk for k ≥ 2, because a leaf's 2k edge-ends saturate its neighbour. Results about k-walks and prisms therefore do not transfer automatically to G[K̄_k].
- "G[K̄_k] Hamiltonian ⇔ G has a connected spanning loopless 2k-regular multigraph on E(G)" is a connected-even-factor (b-matching plus connectivity) condition. Without the connectivity requirement, the existence of such a 2k-regular multigraph is a b-matching / f-factor question and is polynomial. All the hardness lives in the connectivity.

### Gaps
- No toughness-based characterization of Hamiltonicity of G[K̄_k] was found. No paper was found with a "t-tough ⇒ G[K̄_k] Hamiltonian" theorem analogous to the JW Conjecture 2.1 for k-walks or the Kaiser et al. conjecture for prisms.
- No characterization of traceability of G[K̄_k] for general G (open exact-k walks) was found. Only path base graphs (Ekstein–Teska) and Teichert's sufficient conditions are known.
- Exact statements of Teichert's 1983 Theorems 2–3 and his "measures of non-traceability" could not be retrieved.

## Key Question 3: Is deciding Hamiltonicity (or traceability) of G[K̄_k] known to be NP-complete, and what polynomial cases are known?

### Takeaway
For Hamiltonian **cycles**, yes, as an immediate corollary of explicitly stated 1990 results. EXACT k-WALK (closed) is NP-complete for every fixed k (Broersma–Göbel 1990), even restricted to j-connected graphs for any fixed j (Jackson–Wormald 1990, Thm 6.1). Combined with JW's stated equivalence, Hamiltonian Cycle is NP-complete on graphs of the form G[K̄_k], though no paper phrases it in exactly those words. A 2024 preprint sharpens this: [k,k]-HAM is NP-hard on graphs of max degree 3 for every k, with an exact regular-graph dichotomy. For Hamiltonian **paths** in G[K̄_k] (open walks with exactly k visits), no explicit published NP-completeness statement was found. It follows easily, but it is not stated.

### Cited Findings
- JW §6 verbatim: "It [was] shown in [B2] that the problem of whether a [given] graph has an exact k-walk is NP-complete. The proof was by transformation of an arbitrary graph G to a graph G′ such that G has a Hamilton cycle iff G′ has an exact k-walk… In fact, with the proof given, G has a Hamilton cycle iff G′ has any k-walk, and thus the question of whether a given [graph] has a k-walk is NP-complete. However, the graphs G′ have many cut-vertices, and so it is natural to ask whether the restriction of the question to more highly connected graphs is still NP-complete." — [JW90](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/2/ocr-ajc-v2-p135.pdf)
- **JW Theorem 6.1**: "For k and j fixed, K-WALK IN J-CONNECTED GRAPH and EXACT K-WALK IN J-CONNECTED GRAPH are NP-complete." Proof sketch: "form the composition H = G[K_j]. To each inflated vertex of H … join jk − 1 separate [gadgets] … to obtain G′. Then a k-walk in G′ uses at most two edges of H incident with any inflated vertex, and so yields a 1-walk of G… In addition, G′ is j-connected." Some words are illegible in the OCR. The proof itself uses a lexicographic product with a complete graph. — [JW90](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/2/ocr-ajc-v2-p135.pdf)
- JW acknowledgement: Lemma 2.1(i), Theorem 5.1 "and a generalisation of Theorem 6.1" were obtained independently by G. Pruesse, *A Generalization of Hamiltonicity*, M.Sc. thesis, University of Toronto (1990). — [JW90](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/2/ocr-ajc-v2-p135.pdf)
- Liu, Sheffield & Westover, "Complexity of Multiple-Hamiltonicity in Graphs of Bounded Degree" (arXiv:2405.16270, 2024): "In 1990, Broersma and Göbel proposed two variants of the Hamiltonian cycle problem: for fixed k, is there a closed walk that visits each vertex exactly k times, or at least once and at most k times, respectively? They showed that for constant k, both of these problems are NP-hard [BG90]. Jackson and Wormald subsequently proved that both problems remain NP-hard in j-connected graphs for any constant j [JW90]." — [arXiv:2405.16270](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16270)
- **Liu et al. Lemma 1** (attributed to Broersma–Göbel) uses the pendant-triangle gadget: "In unrestricted graphs, for any constants 1 ≤ a ≤ b, [a,b]-HAM is NP-hard. Proof. We reduce from [1,1]-HAM… Given a graph G, we build a new graph G′ by gluing to each vertex b − 1 triangles… In any [a,b]-HAM cycle each of these triangles must be visited — this accounts for b − 1 of the visits for each of the vertices in G, so the rest of the walk when restricted to G must be a Hamiltonian cycle. For the other direction, … satisfy the visit requirements of each triangle by stepping up to it, walking back and forth a − 1 times, and then stepping back down, contributing only 1 additional visit to the vertex we attached the triangles to." They call such gadgets "depletors". — [arXiv:2405.16270](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16270)
- **Liu et al. Theorem 1 (d-regular graphs)**: "[a,b]-HAM is NP-hard if either d is even and b < d/2, or d is odd and b < d. Otherwise, [a,b]-HAM ∈ P." — [arXiv:2405.16270 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.16270v1)
- **Liu et al. Theorem 2 (max degree d)**: "[a,b]-HAM is NP-hard if either a = 1 and b < d, or a > 1 and b/a < d − 1. Otherwise, [a,b]-HAM ∈ P." — [arXiv:2405.16270 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.16270v1)
- Liu et al. Theorems 3–4 (directed): in digraphs of max degree 3, [a,b]-HAM ∈ P if a = b > 1, and NP-hard otherwise. In digraphs of max degree 4 it is NP-hard for all b ≥ a ≥ 1. Lemma 10: linear-time algorithm for [a,b]-HAM on trees. Lemma 9: an [a,b]-HAM labelling can be assumed to have max label ≤ 2a. The authors say "to our knowledge we are the first to consider the algorithmic complexity of [a,b]-HAM in general bounded degree graphs." No journal or conference version was found. — [arXiv:2405.16270](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16270)
- Related complexity results cited by Liu et al. (not independently verified):
  - Ganian et al., "On the complexity landscape of connected f-factor problems", *Algorithmica* 81 (2019) 2606–2632. Liu et al. summarize it as "[a,a]-HAM is NP-hard for a ≤ O(n^{1−ε}), but in RP for a ≥ Ω(n/log n)". Ganian et al. actually study connected f-factors of simple graphs, so this attribution should be checked.
  - Nishiyama et al., "The parity Hamiltonian cycle problem", *Discrete Math.* 341(3) (2018) 606–626: walks visiting every vertex an odd number of times are polynomial, but NP-hard if each edge may be traversed at most 3 times.
  - Schmid & Schmidt, *ACM TALG* 14(2) (2018): polynomial-time 2-walks in 3-connected planar graphs.
  — [arXiv:2405.16270](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16270)
- Mou & Pasechnik, *J. Knot Theory Ramifications* 25(12) (2016) 1642011: every 1/(k−1)-tough 2K₂-free graph has a k-walk (≤ k visits) findable in polynomial time, and (1+ε)-tough 2K₂-free graphs are prism-Hamiltonian with an effective construction. — [zbMATH Zbl 1351.05174](https://zbmath.org/?q=an:1351.05174)

**Other products and prisms (for context)**
- Kaiser, Ryjáček, Král', Rosenfeld & Voss, "Hamilton cycles in prisms", *J. Graph Theory* 56(4) (2007) 249–269: prism-Hamiltonicity (G□K₂) as a relaxation of Hamiltonicity, and a toughness conjecture for prisms. — [zbMATH Zbl 1128.05034](https://zbmath.org/?q=an:1128.05034)

- Grinstead (AJC 95(2), 2026): an infinite family of 2-connected bipartite graphs with 2-walks (τ = 1/2) that are not prism-Hamiltonian. — [AJC v95 p192](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/95/ajc_v95_p192.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Hamiltonian Cycle on G[K̄_k] is NP-complete for every fixed k ≥ 2** (also for k = 1 trivially, and even for G j-connected). This is an immediate corollary of JW90 Theorem 6.1 / BG90 together with JW90's explicit sentence "G has an exact k-walk iff G[K̄_k] has a Hamilton cycle". Its status is "implied / easy corollary of explicitly stated results". JW90 themselves frame the composition connection as a tool ("Thus we may use results on Hamilton cycles to obtain results on k-walks… We exploit a similar connection in examining the complexity of finding k-walks").
- From Liu et al. Theorem 2 with a = b = k: Hamiltonicity of G[K̄_k] is NP-hard already for G of maximum degree 3, for every fixed k ≥ 1. Then b/a = 1 < d − 1 iff d ≥ 3, and for k = 1, b = 1 < d.
- From Liu et al. Theorem 1 with a = b = k: on d-regular G, Hamiltonicity of G[K̄_k] is NP-hard iff (d even and k < d/2) or (d odd and k < d). Otherwise every connected d-regular G works. Example: for connected cubic G, deciding whether G[K̄_2] is Hamiltonian is NP-hard, while G[K̄_k] is always Hamiltonian for k ≥ 3. This is consistent with Kriesell's result that bridgeless cubic G gives Hamiltonian G[K̄_2], so the hard instances must have bridges.
- Liu et al. give no traceability (open-walk) version, and JW90 none either. NP-completeness of Hamiltonian **path** on G[K̄_k], equivalently of "walk visiting every vertex exactly k times", appears not to be stated anywhere I could access. It is very likely folklore-easy: the depletor-triangle argument adapts, or one reduces from the closed version.
- Caveat for an open-walk triangle reduction: a walk's endpoints may lie inside a pendant gadget, which never happens for closed walks. A correct proof for the path version has to handle that case explicitly.
- The pendant-triangle construction for EXACT-K is essentially the same gadget that Liu et al. present as the proof of the Broersma–Göbel hardness lemma. JW90 also describe Broersma's reduction graphs as having "many cut-vertices", consistent with pendant gadgets. So the gadget idea should be regarded as known, at least for closed walks.

### Gaps
- I could not verify whether Broersma–Göbel's original NP-completeness proof used pendant triangles, nor whether it covered open walks.
- NP-completeness of deciding prism-Hamiltonicity (G□K₂): a search-engine summary claimed it is proved NP-complete, but I could not locate or verify a primary source. Kaiser et al. 2007 and Paulraja 1993 full texts were inaccessible (403). Treat as unverified.

## Key Question 4: Is the repo's result already known?

The repo's claim: EXACT-K (walk visiting every vertex exactly k times) is NP-complete for every fixed k ≥ 1 via k−1 pendant triangles on a HAMILTONIAN PATH instance; hence HAM PATH stays NP-complete on G[K̄_k].

### Takeaway
The closed-walk / Hamiltonian-cycle version of every component is already in the literature: the problem, its NP-completeness for each fixed k, the equivalence with Hamiltonicity of G[K̄_k], and the pendant-triangle gadget (via the 2024 retelling). The open-walk / Hamiltonian-path version is, as far as I could find, not explicitly stated, but it is a routine variant. Novelty would be minor at best, and the repo should cite Broersma–Göbel 1990, Jackson–Wormald 1990 and Liu–Sheffield–Westover 2024.

### Cited Findings
- Problem and NP-completeness for each fixed k (closed walks): Broersma & Göbel 1990, as cited by JW90 and Liu et al.; strengthened to j-connected graphs by JW90 Thm 6.1. — [JW90](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/2/ocr-ajc-v2-p135.pdf); [arXiv:2405.16270](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16270)
- Equivalence with Hamilton cycles in G[K̄_k], explicitly stated: JW90 §1. — [JW90](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/2/ocr-ajc-v2-p135.pdf)
- Pendant-triangle ("depletor") gadget reduction from Hamiltonian cycle, b−1 triangles per vertex: Liu et al. Lemma 1, attributed to BG90. — [arXiv:2405.16270](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16270)
- Bounded-degree refinement (max degree 3 suffices for every k): Liu et al. Theorem 2. — [arXiv:2405.16270 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.16270v1)

### Inferences
- Classification for the writer:
  - **(a) Walk ↔ Hamiltonicity of G[K̄_k] equivalence:** EXPLICITLY STATED for closed walks / Hamilton cycles (JW90). For open walks / Hamiltonian paths it is NOT FOUND, though it is an obvious analogue.
  - **(b) Structural characterizations:** the exact k-walk / connected 2k-regular multiple characterization is EXPLICIT (JW90; Kriesell 1997 for the sufficiency direction, general H). The necessary toughness-type condition and monotonicity in k are EXPLICIT (Broersma via JW90). No toughness or f-factor characterization was found.
  - **(c) Complexity:** NP-completeness of Hamiltonian cycle on G[K̄_k] for fixed k is an IMPLIED / EASY COROLLARY of explicit results (BG90, JW90 Thm 6.1 + JW90's equivalence sentence). It holds even for j-connected G and for max-degree-3 G (Liu et al. 2024, as [k,k]-HAM). NP-completeness of Hamiltonian PATH on G[K̄_k], and of open EXACT-K walks, was NOT FOUND explicitly.
- The repo's approach of reducing HAM PATH with k−1 pendant triangles is the open-walk twin of the known closed-walk depletor reduction. If the repo's proof handles walk endpoints that fall inside triangles, it is a correct but minor, folklore-level extension.
