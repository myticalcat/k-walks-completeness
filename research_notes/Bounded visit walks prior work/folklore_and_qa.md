# Bounded-visit walks (EXACT-K / ATMOST-K): folklore, Q&A, textbooks and lesser-known papers

Scope note: the repo under study is `github.com/myticalcat/k-walks-completeness` (confirmed via `git remote -v` of the local checkout). Several web-search hits for "walk visiting every vertex at most twice NP-complete" return **that repo itself**, so they are self-references, not prior work.

The repo's problems use **open** walks (no return to the start) and reduce from HAMILTONIAN PATH. Almost all prior work found uses **closed** walks and reduces from HAMILTONIAN CYCLE. The gadgets match: pendant leaves for "at most k", and pendant triangles (or other non-leaf gadgets) for "exactly k".

Classification legend used below: **[PROOF]** = explicitly stated with a proof or reduction given; **[NO-PROOF]** = stated without proof (or the proof is not accessible to me); **[NOT FOUND]**.

## Q2. "Hamiltonian walk" and k-walk literature: do Goodman–Hedetniemi, Vacek, the Hamiltonian-number papers, or "k-traversable"/"k-walk" papers consider walks with bounded visit counts and their complexity?

### Takeaway
Yes. Both problems were settled in the research literature in 1990, in their **closed-walk** form. Broersma & Göbel ("k-traversable graphs", Ars Combinatoria 29A, 1990) proved that "closed walk visiting each vertex exactly k times" is NP-complete. Their proof also covers "at least once and at most k times". Jackson & Wormald ("k-walks of graphs", Australasian J. Combin. 2, 1990) restated this and extended it to j-connected graphs. For j = 1 their reduction is literally "attach k−1 pendant leaves to every vertex and reduce from HAMILTON CYCLE", the closed-walk version of the repo's ATMOST-K reduction. A 2024 arXiv paper (Liu–Sheffield–Westover) surveys this as known background and extends it to [a,b]-visits, bounded degree and digraphs. The "Hamiltonian walk / Hamiltonian number" literature (Goodman–Hedetniemi 1974, Vacek 1991) studies **minimum-length** spanning walks, not visit-count bounds.

### Cited Findings

**k-walks / k-traversable (closest prior work):**
- **Broersma & Göbel (1990), "k-traversable graphs", Ars Combinatoria 29A, pp. 141–153** (zbMATH Zbl 0722.05045; the review text is not available through zbMATH Open for licensing reasons). **[PROOF; I could not access the original, so the proof is known only through secondary descriptions]** — [zbMATH API record 0722.05045](https://api.zbmath.org/v1/document/_search?search_string=ti%3Ak-traversable%20graphs)
  - Liu et al. describe it as follows: "In 1990, Broersma and Göbel proposed two variants of the Hamiltonian cycle problem: for fixed k, is there a closed walk that visits each vertex exactly k times, or at least once and at most k times, respectively? ... for constant k, both of these problems are NP-hard." — [Liu, Sheffield, Westover, arXiv:2405.16270](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.16270v1)
  - Jackson & Wormald cite it as "[B2] H. Broersma, k-traceable graphs (submitted to Discrete Math.)" and describe its result. Quoted from §6, p. 145: "It was shown in [B2] that the problem of whether a given graph has an exact k-walk is NP-complete. The proof was by transformation of an arbitrary graph G to a graph G′ such that G has a Hamilton cycle iff G′ has an exact k-walk. ... In fact, with the proof given, G has a Hamilton cycle iff G′ has any k-walk, and thus the question of whether a given graph has a k-walk is NP-complete." They also cite [B2, Corollary 3.3]: "if a graph has an exact k-walk then it has an exact (k+1)-walk." — [Jackson & Wormald, AJC 2 (1990) 135–146, PDF](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/2/ocr-ajc-v2-p135.pdf)
  - **Neither secondary source says which gadget Broersma used.**
- **Jackson & Wormald (1990), "k-walks of graphs", Australasian Journal of Combinatorics 2, pp. 135–146.** **[PROOF]** — [PDF (OCR scan)](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/2/ocr-ajc-v2-p135.pdf)
  - Definitions (p. 135): "An exact k-walk (or k-walk) of G is a connected spanning subgraph W of (2k)×G, such that the degree of each vertex v in W is 2k (or is an even number which is at most 2k, respectively)." By Euler's theorem this is the same as a **closed** walk passing through each vertex exactly k times (or at most k times). This is the same "walks as edge multisets" lemma the repo uses, in its closed form.
  - They also note that for k ≥ 2, "G has a k-walk (or exact k-walk) if and only if G[K̄_k] (or G[K_k], respectively) has a Hamilton cycle." (Composition/lexicographic product; the OCR loses the complement bar, so read this as the empty graph on k vertices for k-walks and K_k for exact k-walks.)
  - **Theorem 6.1:** "For k and j fixed, K-WALK IN J-CONNECTED GRAPH and EXACT K-WALK IN J-CONNECTED GRAPH are NP-complete." Proof, quoted verbatim from the page image: "We give a polynomial reduction from HAMILTON CYCLE to each problem. Let G be an arbitrary graph with |V(G)| ≥ 2, and form the composition H = G[K_j]. To each inflated vertex of H ..., join jk − 1 separate copies of K_j to obtain G′. Then a k-walk in G′ uses at most two edges of H incident with any inflated vertex, and so yields a 1-walk of G. The converse also holds. In addition, G′ is j-connected. ... The proof for exact k-walks is exactly the same since if G has a 1-walk it follows that G′ has an exact k-walk."
  - Other results in the paper: Lemma 2.2 says a spanning tree of maximum degree k gives a k-walk (double its edges), and a k-walk gives a spanning tree of maximum degree k+1. Lemma 2.1 gives toughness-type necessary conditions. Conjecture 2.1 says every 1/(k−1)-tough graph has a k-walk. Conjecture 5.1 says every 3-connected planar graph has a 2-walk. — [same PDF](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/2/ocr-ajc-v2-p135.pdf)
  - The acknowledgement says some results, including "a generalisation of Theorem 6.1", were found independently by **G. Pruesse, "A Generalization of Hamiltonicity", M.Sc. thesis, U. of Toronto (1990)**. **[NO-PROOF; thesis not found online]** — [same PDF](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/2/ocr-ajc-v2-p135.pdf). Gara Pruesse's 1992 Toronto PhD is listed in the Math Genealogy Project, but the 1990 M.Sc. thesis is not indexed — [Math Genealogy](https://mathgenealogy.org/id.php?id=103665)
  - Jackson & Wormald also cite **Jungreis (1988)**, "Generalised Hamiltonian circuits in the Cartesian product of two directed cycles", J. Graph Theory 12, 113–120. They describe it as considering "closed walks in Cayley digraph of Z_n ... [visiting some] vertices twice and the rest once" (partly OCR-garbled). — [same PDF](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/2/ocr-ajc-v2-p135.pdf)
- **Liu, Sheffield, Westover (arXiv:2405.16270v1, 25 May 2024), "Complexity of Multiple-Hamiltonicity in Graphs of Bounded Degree".** Defines [a,b]-HAM: "Given integers a, b and graph G, does there exist a closed walk in G that visits every vertex at least a times and at most b times?" Proves it NP-hard for every pair of constants 1 ≤ a ≤ b. [k,k]-HAM is exact-k and [1,k]-HAM is at-most-k. They characterise exactly when the problem is hard in d-regular and bounded-degree graphs using "depletor gadgets", which are forced substructures that use up a vertex's visit budget. They recast the problem as edge labelings with even vertex sums in [2a, 2b] and connected support. **The paper covers closed walks only; open walks are not discussed.** **[PROOF]** — [arXiv:2405.16270](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.16270v1)
  - Main theorems as summarised from the paper: (Thm 1) in d-regular graphs, [a,b]-HAM is NP-hard iff (d even and b < d/2) or (d odd and b < d), and is in P otherwise. (Thm 2) in graphs of max degree d, it is NP-hard iff (a = 1 and b < d) or (a > 1 and b/a < d−1). — [arXiv:2405.16270](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.16270v1)
  - Related work it cites: Gao & Richter 1994, "2-walks in circuit graphs" (JCTB 62(2):259–267), which proves every 3-connected planar graph has a closed walk visiting each vertex once or twice and so settles J&W Conjecture 5.1. Schmid & Schmidt 2018, "Computing 2-Walks in Polynomial Time" (ACM TALG 14(2)). Ganian et al. 2019, connected f-factor problems with non-constant a (Algorithmica 81). Nishiyama et al. 2018, "The parity Hamiltonian cycle problem" (Discrete Math 341(3)). — [arXiv:2405.16270](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.16270v1)

**"Hamiltonian walk" / Hamiltonian number literature (different problem):**
- Goodman & Hedetniemi, "On Hamiltonian Walks in Graphs", SIAM J. Comput. 3 (1974) 214–221, DOI 10.1137/0203017. A Hamiltonian walk is a **closed spanning walk of minimum length**, and its length is the Hamiltonian number h(G). — [SIAM](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/0203017); [MathWorld: Hamiltonian Walk](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/HamiltonianWalk.html)
- MathWorld: for a Hamiltonian graph, h(G) = |G|. — [MathWorld: Hamiltonian Number](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/HamiltonianNumber.html)
- Vacek, "On open Hamiltonian walks in graphs", Archivum Mathematicum 27 (1991) 105–111. An open Hamiltonian walk is a minimum-length open sequence of edges that includes every vertex. The paper studies its properties and upper bounds on its length. Follow-up: "Bounds of lengths of open Hamiltonian walks", Arch. Math. 28 (1992) 11–16. **No visit-count bounds or complexity results appeared in the material available to me** (abstracts via search; the EuDML page returned HTTP 403). — [EuDML 18320](https://eudml.org/doc/18320); [DML-CZ PDF (1991)](https://www.dml.cz/bitstream/handle/10338.dmlcz/107409/ArchMath_027-1991-1_13.pdf); [DML-CZ PDF (1992)](https://www.dml.cz/bitstream/handle/10338.dmlcz/107431/ArchMathRetro_028-1992-1_3.pdf)
- "k-traceable graphs" in the later literature has an unrelated meaning: every induced subgraph of order k has a Hamilton path. Do not confuse it with Broersma's 1989/90 manuscript title "k-traceable graphs". — [Electronic J. Combin. 18 (2011) P63](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/download/v18i1p63/pdf/)
- Jiang (arXiv:2208.02153, Aug 2022), "Finding a Lower Bound for k-Unbounded Hamiltonian Cycles". It counts how many vertices must be revisited (unbounded number of revisits) and gives brute-force and heuristic algorithms. It does not cite Broersma or Jackson–Wormald. — [arXiv:2208.02153](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.02153)

### Inferences
- **Closed-walk analogues of both repo claims are 36-year-old published results.** ATMOST-K (closed) = J&W "k-walk", and EXACT-K (closed) = J&W "exact k-walk" / Broersma–Göbel "k-traversable". Both were NP-complete by 1990. Taking j = 1 in J&W's Theorem 6.1 gives "H = G, and join k−1 copies of K_1 (pendant leaves) to each vertex, reducing from HAMILTON CYCLE". That is the repo's ATMOST-K gadget, with HAMILTONIAN PATH replaced by HAMILTON CYCLE.
- **Possible flaw in J&W's exact case at j = 1 (my own check, not stated in any source).** In an exact k-walk, a pendant leaf needs degree 2k, so its single edge has multiplicity 2k. That alone uses the neighbour's entire degree budget of 2k, so for k ≥ 2 and |V(G)| ≥ 2 the walk would be disconnected. So "the proof for exact k-walks is exactly the same" does not hold for j = 1 with k−1 pendant K_1's. It looks correct for j ≥ 2, where each attached copy of K_j can be visited internally. Broersma's original result still covers general graphs, through an unspecified construction. This matches the repo's choice of **pendant triangles** (not leaves) for EXACT-K, and the CS/ECE 374 solution below, which uses a triangle for exactly-twice.
- **Open walks.** I found no source that explicitly states the open-walk (path) versions. The adaptation looks routine: reduce from HAMILTONIAN PATH with the same gadgets, and handle the endpoint degree exceptions 2K−1 / 2K−2. So the repo's results are best described as the open-walk counterparts of known closed-walk results. They are unlikely to count as new complexity results.
- The minimum-length Hamiltonian-walk literature (h(G)) is a different optimisation problem. NP-hardness of computing h(G) follows trivially from h(G) = n ⇔ Hamiltonian, but it says nothing about bounded visit counts.

### Gaps
- I could not access the full text of Broersma & Göbel (Ars Combin. 29A, 1990). The zbMATH review is withheld, and Broersma's publication page returned HTTP 403. Their exact gadget and whether they also treat open walks ("k-traceable" in the manuscript title suggests possibly) are **unverified**. Broersma's 1988 Twente PhD thesis may contain the same material; not checked.
- Pruesse's 1990 Toronto M.Sc. thesis ("A Generalization of Hamiltonicity"), cited by J&W as an independent generalisation of Theorem 6.1, was not found online.
- I did not verify whether Asano–Nishizeki–Watanabe (shortest Hamiltonian walks in maximal planar graphs) or the "Hamiltonian spectrum" papers mention visit bounds. Only the Goodman–Hedetniemi and Vacek abstracts and definitions were checked.
- I did not verify whether Liu et al. 2405.16270 has a later journal or conference version.

## Q3. Puzzles and textbook or course exercises posing "visit every vertex exactly twice / at most twice is NP-hard"

### Takeaway
This is a recurring homework exercise. Jeff Erickson's *Algorithms* (Ch. 12) and his UIUC lecture notes (CS 573 Fall 2010) pose "double-Hamiltonian tour/circuit" (closed walk, every vertex exactly twice) and "triple-Hamiltonian tour" as exercises. UIUC CS/ECE 374 (Spring 2017, HW10) publishes a **full solution** for exactly-twice using a **pendant triangle** on every vertex, reducing from Hamiltonian cycle. MIT 6.890 (Demaine, Fall 2014, PS3) publishes a full solution for at-most-twice using a triangle, and notes that a **single pendant leaf** also works. These are the closed-walk forms of the repo's EXACT-2 and ATMOST-2 reductions, with the same gadgets. I found no grid-puzzle version.

### Cited Findings
- **Erickson, CS 573 Lecture 30 "NP-Hard Problems" [Fa'10], Exercise 15** **[NO-PROOF: exercise only]**: "For each problem below, either describe a polynomial-time algorithm or prove that the problem is NP-complete. (a) A double-Eulerian circuit in an undirected graph G is a closed walk that traverses every edge in G exactly twice. ... (b) A double-Hamiltonian circuit in an undirected graph G is a closed walk that visits every vertex in G exactly twice. Given a graph G, does G have a double-Hamiltonian circuit?" — [UIUC CS 573 Fall 2010 notes](https://courses.grainger.illinois.edu/cs573/fa2010/notes/30-nphard.pdf)
- **Erickson, *Algorithms* (1st ed., 2019), Chapter 12 "NP-Hardness", exercise list** **[NO-PROOF: exercise only]**: "For each of the following problems, either describe a polynomial-time algorithm or prove that the problem is NP-hard. (a) A double-Eulerian tour ... traverses every edge in G exactly twice. (b) A double-Hamiltonian tour in an undirected graph G is a closed walk that visits every vertex in G exactly twice. ... (c) A double-Hamiltonian circuit ... is a closed walk that visits every vertex in G exactly twice and traverses each edge in G at most once. (d) A triple-Eulerian tour ... traverses every edge in G exactly three times. (e) A triple-Hamiltonian tour in an undirected graph G is a closed walk that visits every vertex in G exactly three times." Text extraction lost the exercise number. One search snippet called it exercise 32, which I did not verify. — [Erickson, Algorithms, Ch. 12 PDF](https://jeffe.cs.illinois.edu/teaching/algorithms/book/12-nphard.pdf)
- **UIUC CS/ECE 374, Spring 2017, Homework 10 (due 28 Apr 2017), Solved Problem 4** **[PROOF]**: "A double-Hamiltonian tour in an undirected graph G is a closed walk that visits every vertex in G exactly twice. Prove that it is NP-hard..." — [CS/ECE 374 Sp17 HW10](https://courses.grainger.illinois.edu/cs374/sp2017/homework/hw10.pdf)
  - Reduction, quoted: "We construct a new graph H by attaching a small gadget to every vertex of G. Specifically, for each vertex v, we add two vertices v♯ and v♭, along with three edges vv♭, vv♯, and v♭v♯." This is a **pendant triangle**, the repo's EXACT-K gadget with K = 2.
  - Forward direction: replace each v_i in the Hamiltonian cycle by "v_i → v_i♭ → v_i♯ → v_i".
  - Converse, quoted: "the tour D must visit v exactly twice. Those two visits split D into two closed walks, each of which visits v exactly once. Any walk from v♭ or v♯ to any other vertex in H must pass through v. Thus, one of the two closed walks visits only the vertices v, v♭, and v♯. Thus, if we simply remove the vertices in H \ G from D, we obtain a closed walk in G that visits every vertex in G once."
  - It also mentions a 5-vertex, 11-edge gadget for the version where each edge is used at most once. It flags a "common incorrect solution (self-loops)" with a counterexample graph that has a double-Hamiltonian tour but no Hamiltonian cycle. This is the same phenomenon the repo's README notes on raw graphs.
- **MIT 6.890 "Algorithmic Lower Bounds" (Prof. Erik Demaine; TAs Sarah Eisenstat, Jayson Lynch), Fall 2014, Problem Set 3 (due 22 Oct 2014), Problem 1(a) "A Tour of Hamiltonicity Variants"** **[PROOF]**: "Given a simple graph, is there a cycle that visits every node at least once and no more than twice?" Here "cycle" means closed walk. — [MIT 6.890 F14 PS3 solutions](https://courses.csail.mit.edu/6.890/fall14/psets/ps3-solutions.pdf); also on [MIT OCW](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-890-algorithmic-lower-bounds-fun-with-hardness-proofs-fall-2014/cd950adceafafac5d92e4ad0f0e4244d_MIT6_890F14_ps3-solutions.pdf)
  - Solution, quoted: "We will prove this is NP-hard by a simple reduction from Hamiltonian Cycle. We take the original input graph and to every vertex we attach two extra nodes in a triangle. ... To visit the auxiliary nodes, we are forced to visit each node twice, once to enter the triangle and once to exit. Thus if there is not a Hamiltonian Cycle, we would either be forced to skip a triangle or visit an original node more than once. **Many of the given solutions add a single leaf to every node. This is also correct**, and it is interesting to note that this reduction is parsimonious, whereas the triangle gadget is not, since each can be traversed in two different directions."

### Inferences
- The closed versions of EXACT-2 and ATMOST-2, with exactly the repo's gadgets (triangle and leaf), were standard teaching material by at least 2010–2017. Demaine's course noting that "many" students independently found the leaf gadget shows how natural it is.
- The CS/ECE 374 converse argument (two visits to v split the closed walk, and one part is confined to the gadget) is the closed-walk analogue of what the repo needs for EXACT-K with K−1 triangles. The repo's open-walk version must also handle the walk's endpoints lying inside a gadget.

## Q4. Directed-graph versions and edge-visit versions

### Takeaway
Directed versions with closed walks are fully characterised for bounded degree by Liu–Sheffield–Westover (2024). In directed graphs of max degree 3, the exact-k problem with k > 1 is **in P**, while the a ≠ b and k = 1 cases are NP-hard. At max degree 4 every [a,b] combination is NP-hard. Edge-visit variants appear as textbook exercises (double/triple-Eulerian tours, double-Hamiltonian circuits with each edge used at most once) and in an MIT problem on directed walks that visit every node but no edge twice. I found no source on open directed walks with vertex-visit bounds.

### Cited Findings
- Liu et al., Theorem 3 (directed, max degree 3): "[a,b]-HAM ∈ P if a = b > 1, and NP-hard otherwise." Theorem 4 (directed, max degree 4): "For all b ≥ a ≥ 1, [a,b]-HAM is NP-hard." (Closed walks.) **[PROOF]** — [arXiv:2405.16270](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.16270v1)
- Jungreis (1988) studied closed walks in Cayley digraphs of Z_n × Z_m (the Cartesian product of two directed cycles) that visit some vertices twice and the rest once, as cited by Jackson & Wormald. This is structural, not complexity. — [Jackson & Wormald 1990, intro and ref. [J]](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/2/ocr-ajc-v2-p135.pdf)
- MIT 6.890 F14 PS3 1(b), an edge-bounded open walk in a digraph: "Given a simple directed graph, is there a path that visits every node at least once, but does not cross an edge more than once?" NP-hard by reduction from directed Hamiltonian path: "We replace each node with two nodes, one with all the incoming edges and one with all the outgoing ones, connected by an edge. Then, if we ever return to a node we cannot recross the edge to the outgoing node and thus cannot leave this node." **[PROOF]** — [MIT 6.890 F14 PS3 solutions](https://courses.csail.mit.edu/6.890/fall14/psets/ps3-solutions.pdf)
- Erickson's exercises on edge-visit counts: "double-Eulerian tour" (closed walk traversing every edge exactly twice), "triple-Eulerian tour" (exactly three times), and "double-Hamiltonian circuit" (every vertex exactly twice, each edge at most once). These are posed as "polynomial-time or NP-hard?" with no answer in the book chapter. **[NO-PROOF]** — [Erickson, Algorithms Ch. 12](https://jeffe.cs.illinois.edu/teaching/algorithms/book/12-nphard.pdf). The CS/ECE 374 Sp17 solution says a 5-vertex, 11-edge gadget proves NP-hardness of the double-Hamiltonian circuit version (each edge at most once), but the figure was not extractable as text. **[PROOF, gadget only in figure]** — [CS/ECE 374 Sp17 HW10](https://courses.grainger.illinois.edu/cs374/sp2017/homework/hw10.pdf)
- Nishiyama et al. (2018), "The parity Hamiltonian cycle problem", Discrete Math 341(3):606–626, as summarised by Liu et al.: closed walks visiting each vertex an odd number of times can be found in polynomial time, but the problem becomes NP-hard when each edge may be traversed at most 3 times. — [arXiv:2405.16270 related-work section](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.16270v1)

### Inferences
- The directed max-degree-3 result shows that exact-k for k > 1 can be **easier** than Hamiltonicity in restricted classes. Bounded-visit walk problems do not uniformly inherit Hamiltonicity's hardness. This is relevant if the repo wants to claim anything about restricted graph classes.
- The repo's results are for general undirected graphs and open walks. The nearest directed analogue found (MIT 1(b)) bounds edge visits, not vertex visits.

### Gaps
- I found no source on **open** directed walks visiting every vertex exactly or at most k times. It presumably follows from directed Hamiltonian path with analogous gadgets, but no source says so.
- I did not obtain the actual gadget figure for the CS/ECE 374 "each edge at most once" variant, or a solution to Erickson's triple-Eulerian/triple-Hamiltonian parts.
- "k-fold Hamiltonian" as a search phrase was not run separately. "Double trace" papers (closed walks traversing every edge exactly twice, e.g., arXiv:1610.09888 "E-restricted Double Traces") surfaced in results but were not read.
