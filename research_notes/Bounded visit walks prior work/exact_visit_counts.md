# Exact visit counts: prior work on walks/tours that visit every vertex an exact number of times, and the edge-multiplicity (Euler) characterization

Scope note: this file covers the "exactly K visits" problem (EXACT-K), the Many-Visits TSP (MV-TSP) literature, and connected-factor / connected-multigraph formulations. The adjacent "at most k" (k-walk) literature and lexicographic-product approaches are covered in sibling files (`k_walks_atmost.md`, `lexicographic_products.md`). They are touched on here only where they bear on EXACT-K.

Labels used below: **[EXPLICIT]** means the statement appears in the cited source. **[COROLLARY]** means it follows immediately from statements in the source but is not stated as such. **[NOT FOUND]** means it was not located in the sources checked.

## Q0. Are the repo's claims (1)–(3) already known?

### Takeaway
Yes, largely. Claim (3), the closed walk, is explicitly published: Broersma & Göbel (1990), Jackson & Wormald (1990) and Liu–Sheffield–Westover (2024). Liu et al. use exactly the repo's gadget: glue b−1 = K−1 triangles to every vertex and reduce from HAMILTONIAN CYCLE. Claim (2), the Euler/multiplicity lemma, is explicit in several sources: Jackson–Wormald for closed walks, Berger–Kozma–Mnich–Vincze for directed closed tours, and Bérczi–Mnich–Vincze for open s–t walks with degree 2r(v)−1 at s and t. Claim (1), the open walk reduced from HAMILTONIAN PATH, was not found stated explicitly for general graphs. It is a direct adaptation of the published closed-walk reduction. NP membership with K in binary is an immediate corollary of the compact multiplicity-vector representation that the MV-TSP literature uses throughout.

### Cited Findings
- **[EXPLICIT, closed EXACT-K, fixed k]** Liu, Sheffield and Westover state: "In 1990, Broersma and Göbel proposed two variants of the Hamiltonian cycle problem: for fixed k, is there a closed walk that visits each vertex exactly k times, or at least once and at most k times, respectively? They showed that for constant k, both of these problems are NP-hard [BG90]." — [Liu, Sheffield, Westover, "Complexity of Multiple-Hamiltonicity in Graphs of Bounded Degree", arXiv:2405.16270 (May 2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16270)
- **[EXPLICIT]** The Broersma–Göbel citation, as given by Liu et al.: H.J. Broersma and F. Göbel, "k-Traversable graphs", *Ars Combinatoria* 29A (June 1990), pp. 141–153. A graph is "k-traversable" if it admits a closed walk in which every vertex occurs exactly k times. — [arXiv:2405.16270 bibliography](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16270). The primary paper was not accessed directly; see Gaps.
- **[EXPLICIT, same gadget as the repo]** Liu et al. Lemma 1, attributed to Broersma and Göbel [BG90]: "In unrestricted graphs, for any constants 1 ≤ a ≤ b, [a,b]-HAM is NP-hard." Their proof: "We reduce from [1,1]-HAM (i.e. the standard Hamiltonian cycle problem). Given a graph G, we build a new graph G′ by gluing to each vertex b − 1 triangles … In any [a,b]-HAM cycle each of these triangles must be visited — this accounts for b − 1 of the visits for each of the vertices in G, so the rest of the walk when restricted to G must be a Hamiltonian cycle. For the other direction … satisfy the visit requirements of each triangle by stepping up to it, walking back and forth a − 1 times, and then stepping back down, contributing only 1 additional visit to the vertex." Here [a,b]-HAM means a closed walk visiting each vertex at least a and at most b times, so a = b = K is EXACT-K. — [arXiv:2405.16270, §1.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16270)
- **[EXPLICIT, closed EXACT-K, NP-complete even in j-connected graphs]** Jackson & Wormald, §6, "NP-completeness of k-walk problems": "It was shown in [B2] that the problem of whether a given graph has an exact k-walk is NP-complete. The proof was by transformation of an arbitrary graph G to a graph G′ such that G has a Hamilton cycle iff G′ has an exact k-walk … the graphs G′ have many cut-vertices." Theorem 6.1: "For k and j fixed, K-WALK IN J-CONNECTED GRAPH and EXACT K-WALK IN J-CONNECTED GRAPH are NP-complete." The proof composes H = G[K_j] and joins jk−1 separate copies of K_j to each inflated vertex. In other words it also uses pendant clique gadgets. — [B. Jackson, N.C. Wormald, "k-walks of graphs", Australasian J. Combinatorics 2 (1990) 135–146](https://ajc-new.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/2/ajc-v2-p135.pdf) ([OCR version](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/2/ocr-ajc-v2-p135.pdf))
- **[EXPLICIT]** In Jackson–Wormald, [B2] is cited as "H. Broersma, k-traceable graphs (submitted to Discrete Math.)". This appears to be the preprint of the Broersma–Göbel Ars Combinatoria paper. They also acknowledge that G. Pruesse, *A Generalization of Hamiltonicity*, M.Sc. thesis, U. Toronto (1990), independently obtained "a generalisation of Theorem 6.1". — [Jackson & Wormald 1990, p. 145–146](https://ajc-new.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/2/ajc-v2-p135.pdf)
- **[NOT FOUND]** No open-walk / HAMILTONIAN PATH version of EXACT-K on general graphs was located. Liu et al. state that they study closed walks only. Jackson–Wormald and Broersma–Göbel (as described by others) are also closed-walk. — [arXiv:2405.16270](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16270); [Jackson & Wormald 1990](https://ajc-new.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/2/ajc-v2-p135.pdf)

### Inferences
- The repo's claim (3) is essentially a rediscovery of Broersma–Göbel (1990) and Jackson–Wormald (1990) Theorem 6.1. The repo's specific "K−1 pendant triangles at every vertex" gadget appears verbatim in Liu–Sheffield–Westover's Lemma 1 proof, which they attribute to Broersma–Göbel.
- Claim (1), the open walk reduced from HAMILTONIAN PATH, is a natural variant. A proof must handle one wrinkle that the closed case avoids: an open walk may start or end inside a pendant gadget. It is therefore a modest adaptation rather than verbatim prior art. A paper would most likely present it as "the standard reduction [BG90/JW90], adapted to paths".
- Claim (2) for the closed case is stated outright by Jackson–Wormald ("exact k-walk" defined as a connected spanning 2k-regular subgraph of (2k)×G). The open-walk version with two vertices of degree 2K−1 matches Bérczi–Mnich–Vincze's feasibility condition (see Q2). The repo's "either two vertices of degree 2K−1 or one of degree 2K−2" disjunction is a routine combination of the Euler-trail and Euler-circuit cases.

### Gaps
- The Broersma–Göbel paper (Ars Combin. 29A, 1990) was not accessible. Its exact theorem numbering, its gadget, and whether it also treats an open/"traceable" variant are unverified. Jackson–Wormald's preprint title "k-traceable graphs" hints that a path variant may have been considered, but this is speculation.
- Pruesse's 1990 M.Sc. thesis was not located online.

## Q1. Many-Visits TSP: formulation, behaviour on non-complete graphs (infinite costs), and any NP-completeness for fixed r(v) = k

### Takeaway
The MV-TSP literature formulates the problem as "visit each city v exactly k(v) (or r(v)) times", with k(v) given in binary. Several papers explicitly allow infinite costs, d ∈ N ∪ {∞}, so the decision version on a general (di)graph is exactly closed-walk feasibility with prescribed visit counts. These papers note hardness only through the k ≡ 1 special case (TSP / directed Hamiltonicity). None of the MV-TSP papers checked state NP-completeness for fixed k ≥ 2 on general graphs. Their contribution is algorithms exponential in n but only logarithmic in Σk.

### Cited Findings
- **Cosmadakis & Papadimitriou.** "The traveling salesman problem with many visits to few cities", *SIAM J. Comput.* 13(1):99–108, 1984. The tech-memo version is MIT-LCS-TM-208, Nov 1981. Abstract: a version of TSP "in which a relatively small number of cities -- say, six -- must be visited a huge number of times", noting "it costs to go from one city to itself". The algorithm's "running time is exponential in the number of cities, but logarithmic in the number of visits." — [MIT DSpace, MIT-LCS-TM-208](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/149019); SICOMP citation as given in [Kowalik et al., arXiv:2005.02329](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.02329)
- Kowalik et al. on Cosmadakis–Papadimitriou: the algorithm runs in "time 2^{O(n log n)} + O(n^3 log ℓ)". "Beginning from the work of Cosmadakis and Papadimitriou we consider MVTSP with compressed output, namely the output is a multiplicity function which encodes the number of times every edge is visited by the tour. By using a standard Eulerian tour algorithm we can compute an explicit tour from this output." The crux of their approach was "an observation that every solution can be decomposed to a minimal connected spanning Eulerian subgraph … and subgraph satisfying appropriate degree constraints". — [Kowalik, Li, Nadara, Smulewicz, Wahlström, "Many visits TSP revisited", arXiv:2005.02329 (ESA 2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.02329)
- **Kowalik et al. definition [EXPLICIT, infinite costs allowed]:** "we are given a set V of n vertices, with pairwise distances (or costs) d : V² → Z≥0 ∪ {∞}. We are also given a function k : V → Z+. A valid tour of length ℓ is a sequence of vertices (x1,…,xℓ), where ℓ = Σ k(v), such that each v ∈ V appears in the sequence exactly k(v) times." — [arXiv:2005.02329](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.02329)
- **[EXPLICIT, unweighted feasibility = general-graph EXACT-k(v)]** Kowalik et al.: "Observe that if the image of d is {0,+∞} we get the natural unweighted version, where we are given a graph with edge set d⁻¹(0) and the goal is to decide if one can choose multiplicities of the edges so that the resulting digraph contains a member of F and its in- and outdegrees match the demands." They define "Decision Unweighted Fixed Degree Connected Subgraph" (input: digraph G, in, out : V → Z≥0; question: is there m such that the support of m is a connected subgraph of G with in/out-degrees equal to the demands?). They note it "generalizes the directed Hamiltonian cycle problem". Theorem 4.1: a randomized algorithm solves it "in time O*(2^n poly(M)) and polynomial space, where M = max_v max{in(v), out(v)}" (Monte Carlo, one-sided error). — [arXiv:2005.02329, §3–4.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.02329)
- Kowalik et al. main results: a randomized O*(2^n D)-time polynomial-space algorithm (D = max finite distance), a tight O*(4^n) analysis of Berger et al.'s algorithm, and an O(7.88^n) polynomial-space algorithm. "Improving the constant 2 … would result in improving the O*(2^n)-time algorithm for Directed Hamiltonian Cycle." — [arXiv:2005.02329](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.02329)
- **Berger, Kozma, Mnich, Vincze.** "Time- and space-optimal algorithm for the many-visits TSP", SODA 2019, pp. 1770–1782; ACM Trans. Algorithms 2020. Definition [EXPLICIT]: "pairwise distances (or costs) d_ij ∈ N ∪ {∞} … the cost d_ii of a self-loop may be non-zero. Also given are integers k_i ≥ 1 … A valid tour … such that each i ∈ V appears in the sequence exactly k_i times." On hardness, only: "MV-TSP includes the standard metric TSP … [so] unless P = NP, it cannot be solved in polynomial time." Applications listed include "Hamiltonicity of certain graph families", citing Lampis, Algorithmica 64 (2012) 19–37, on neighborhood diversity. — [arXiv:1804.06361](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06361)
- **[EXPLICIT]** Berger et al. note: "As it is NP-complete to test the non-minimality of an Eulerian digraph [Papadimitriou & Yannakakis, IPL 12(4), 1981]", which is why Cosmadakis–Papadimitriou relax minimality. — [arXiv:1804.06361](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06361)
- **Bérczi, Mnich, Vincze.** "A 3/2-Approximation for the Metric Many-visits Path TSP", EGRES TR-2020-19; journal version in SIAM J. Discrete Math. per repository listings, volume not verified. Problem: "a minimum cost s-t-walk that visits each city v exactly r(v) times, where leaving city s as well as arriving to city t counts as one visit." Costs are metric on a complete graph with a self-loop at every vertex, so feasibility is never an issue there. Results: 5/2-approximation (Thm 1), 3/2-approximation (Thm 2) and 3/2 for the cyclic MV-TSP (Cor 3), all "polynomial in n and log r". History given: Rothkopf (1966), Psaraftis (1980), Cosmadakis–Papadimitriou (1984), Berger et al., Kowalik et al. — [EGRES TR-2020-19](https://egres.elte.hu/tr/egres-20-19.pdf)
- Bérczi, Mnich, Vincze, "Approximations for many-visits multiple traveling salesman problems", *Omega* 116 (2023) 102816, doi:10.1016/j.omega.2022.102816. It treats the many-visits mTSP, where each city v has request r(v). It is an approximation paper; no general-graph feasibility hardness was seen in the listing. — [IDEAS/RePEc listing](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jomega/v116y2023ics0305048322002225.html)
- Grigoriev & van de Klundert, "On the high multiplicity traveling salesman problem", *Discrete Optimization* 3(1):50–62, 2006. The abstract concerns how optimal tours change when all visit counts are multiplied by a common factor. Per Kowalik et al. and Berger et al., they "describe an algorithm which transforms [a multiplicity function] to a compressed representation of the tour in time O(n⁴ log ℓ)". — [Maastricht CRIS](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/en/publications/on-the-high-multiplicity-traveling-salesman-problem/); [arXiv:2005.02329](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.02329)
- Brauner, Crama, Grigoriev, van de Klundert, "A framework for the complexity of high-multiplicity scheduling problems", *J. Comb. Optim.* 9(3):313–323, 2005. It proposes complexity classes for high-multiplicity problems with exponentially long outputs. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10878-005-1414-7)

### Inferences
- **[COROLLARY]** Undirected EXACT-K closed-walk feasibility on a graph G is MV-TSP with symmetric d ∈ {0, ∞}, d(v,v) = ∞ (no loops), and k ≡ K. So the MV-TSP exact algorithms apply directly: O*(4^n)-type deterministic and O*(2^n poly(K)) randomized algorithms (Kowalik et al., directed setting), with only log K dependence in Cosmadakis–Papadimitriou and Berger et al.
- **[COROLLARY]** Since MV-TSP solutions are represented as multiplicity vectors of size O(n² log Σk), checkable for connectivity and degrees in polynomial time, NP membership of feasibility with binary k(v) follows immediately. The MV-TSP papers use this representation as standard but do not phrase it as an "∈ NP" theorem in the passages checked.
- No MV-TSP paper checked states NP-hardness of feasibility for fixed k ≥ 2. They rely on k ≡ 1 (Hamiltonicity/TSP). The fixed-k hardness lives in the graph-theory literature (Q0).

### Gaps
- The full SICOMP text of Cosmadakis–Papadimitriou was not read: the DSpace PDF download was blocked. Whether it states an explicit "∈ NP" observation or discusses forbidden edges is unverified.
- The coordinator mentioned "Kannan, Lenstra et al." No source was found linking Kannan/Lenstra-style fixed-dimension ILP to the many-visits feasibility problem. Not investigated further.
- Exact volume and pages for the SIAM J. Discrete Math. version of the Bérczi–Mnich–Vincze path paper, and the JCSS version of Kowalik et al., were not verified.

## Q2. Edge-multiplicity / connected Eulerian multigraph formulation (connected f-factors, connected degree-constrained multigraphs)

### Takeaway
The "multiplicity function with connected spanning support and prescribed even degrees" characterization is standard and explicit. It appears for closed undirected walks (Jackson–Wormald 1990; Liu et al. 2024), directed closed tours (Berger et al. Lemma 2.1; Kowalik et al. Observation 2.1) and open s–t walks (Bérczi–Mnich–Vincze). The simple-subgraph analogue (connected f-factors, no repeated edges) is a different problem with its own hardness literature (Cheah–Corneil 1990; Cornelissen et al. 2013/2018; Ganian et al. 2016/2019).

### Cited Findings
- **[EXPLICIT, closed, undirected]** Jackson & Wormald: "denote by k×G the multigraph obtained from G by multiplying all edges by k. An exact k-walk (or k-walk) of G is a connected spanning subgraph W of (2k)×G, such that the degree of each vertex v in W is 2k (or is an even number which is at most 2k, respectively). This nomenclature is motivated by the fact that Euler's Theorem implies that a graph with a k-walk (or exact k-walk) possesses a closed walk passing through each vertex at most k times (or exactly k times, respectively)." — [Jackson & Wormald 1990, p. 135](https://ajc-new.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/2/ajc-v2-p135.pdf)
- **[EXPLICIT]** Jackson & Wormald also note: "for k ≥ 2, G has a k-walk (or exact k-walk) if and only if G[K_k] (or G[K̄_k], respectively) has a Hamilton cycle". Here G[H] is the composition (lexicographic product). They cite Broersma: "if a graph has an exact k-walk then it has an exact (k+1)-walk (k ≥ 1)" [B2, Cor. 3.3]. — [Jackson & Wormald 1990, p. 135](https://ajc-new.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/2/ajc-v2-p135.pdf)
- **[EXPLICIT]** Liu et al.: "G is [a,b]-HAM if and only if 2b·G contains a spanning even [2a,2b] factor; that is, a connected subgraph containing all vertices of G, with every degree even and in [2a,2b]. The equivalence of this formulation follows from taking an Euler tour." In labelling form: label each edge by its traversal count, so "the sum of the labels on the edges adjacent to each vertex is an even number at least 2a and at most 2b, and the subgraph on edges with non-zero labels is connected". Lemma 3 (attributed to Broersma–Göbel): "If a graph G is [b,b]-HAM for some b, then it's also [b′,b′]-HAM for all b′ > b". The proof applies Petersen/Tutte's 2-factorization of a 2b-regular multigraph and doubles one 2-factor. — [arXiv:2405.16270](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16270)
- **[EXPLICIT, directed, closed, infinite costs allowed]** Berger et al. Lemma 2.1: "Let G be a directed multigraph over V … Then G is the edge set of a tour that visits each vertex i ∈ V exactly k_i times if and only if (i) the underlying graph of G is connected, and (ii) for all i ∈ V, δ_out(i) = δ_in(i) = k_i." The proof cites "the well-known 'Euler's theorem'". — [arXiv:1804.06361](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06361)
- **[EXPLICIT, directed]** Kowalik et al. Observation 2.1: "Many Visits TSP has a tour of cost c if and only if there is a multiplicity function m_G : V² → Z≥0 of cost c such that m contains a spanning connected subgraph" (degree conditions implicit in their definition of solution). Observation 3.1: MV-TSP is a special case of Fixed Degree Connected Subgraph with in(v) = out(v) = k(v). — [arXiv:2005.02329](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.02329)
- **[EXPLICIT, open s–t walk]** Bérczi–Mnich–Vincze: "a vector z ∈ Z^E_{≥0} represents a feasible tour if supp(z) is a connected subgraph of G and deg_z(v) = 2·r(v) holds for all v ∈ V − {s,t} and deg_z(v) = 2·r(v) − 1 for v ∈ {s,t}. (Note that each self-loop vv contributes 2 …)". This is the repo's "two vertices of degree 2K−1" case, but on a complete graph with self-loops. They also define "Minimum Bounded Degree Connected Multigraph with Edge Bounds" and give an algorithm polynomial in n and log Σρ(v) returning a connected multigraph with degree ≥ ρ(v)−1 and cost ≤ LP optimum (Thm 4). — [EGRES TR-2020-19](https://egres.elte.hu/tr/egres-20-19.pdf)
- **[EXPLICIT, simple-subgraph analogue]** Cornelissen, Hoeksma, Manthey, Narayanaswamy, Rahul: "Cheah and Corneil [6] have shown that deciding whether a given graph G = (V,E) has a d-regular connected spanning subgraph is NP-complete for every d ≥ 2, where d = 2 is just the Hamiltonian cycle problem." Cheah–Corneil's reduction attaches a "d-expansion" gadget (K_{d+1} minus a matching of size ⌈d/2⌉−1, joined to v) to every vertex. They extend hardness to d = Θ(n^{1−ε}) for even d (Thm 4.5) and show d-RCS ∈ P for d ≥ n/2 − 1 (Thm 5.2). — [Cornelissen et al., "Approximability of Connected Factors", arXiv:1310.2387 (WAOA 2013)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.2387). The journal version is "Approximation Algorithms for Connected Graph Factors of Minimum Weight" (with Waanders), Theory Comput. Syst. 62(2):441–464, 2018 — [U. Twente listing](https://research.utwente.nl/en/publications/approximation-algorithms-for-connected-graph-factors-of-minimum-w/). Original: F. Cheah, D.G. Corneil, "The complexity of regular subgraph recognition", Discrete Applied Math. 27(1–2):59–68, 1990 (citation via Cornelissen et al.).
- **[EXPLICIT, simple graphs]** Ganian, Narayanaswamy, Ordyniak, Rahul, Ramanujan, "On the complexity landscape of connected f-factor problems", MFCS 2016; Algorithmica 81(6):2606–2632, 2019. "Let G be an undirected simple graph … checking for the presence of a connected f-factor is easily seen to generalize HAMILTONIAN CYCLE and hence is NP-complete … remains NP-complete even when we restrict f(v) to be at least n^ε … polynomial time solvable when f(v) is at least n/3." New results: for f(v) ≥ n/(log n)^c, quasi-polynomial time, and randomized polynomial time if c ≤ 1. For c > 1 the problem is "NP-intermediate" under ETH. — [White Rose accepted version](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/143876/1/journal-factor.pdf)
- Liu et al. summarize Ganian et al. as "[a,a]-HAM is NP-hard for a ≤ O(n^{1−ε}), but in RP for a ≥ Ω(n/log n)". However, Ganian et al.'s problem is for simple subgraphs (connected f-factors), not multigraph walks. — [arXiv:2405.16270](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16270) vs [Ganian et al.](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/143876/1/journal-factor.pdf)

### Inferences
- A connected 2K-regular spanning simple subgraph is the same as a spanning closed trail (no repeated edge) visiting each vertex exactly K times. So Cheah–Corneil (1990) already gives NP-completeness for fixed K of the trail version. The walk version (multiplicities allowed) is the Broersma–Göbel / Jackson–Wormald problem. The two differ and should not be conflated. Neither directly implies the other, although both reductions use per-vertex pendant gadgets.
- Liu et al.'s gloss of Ganian et al. as an [a,a]-HAM result looks imprecise: simple versus multigraph. A careful write-up should cite Ganian et al. only for the simple connected f-factor problem.
- Jackson–Wormald's equivalence "exact k-walk ⇔ G[K̄_k] Hamiltonian (k ≥ 2)" is a second, product-based route to both hardness and structure. See `lexicographic_products.md`.

### Gaps
- No paper was found titled or framed as "Euler tours with prescribed vertex multiplicities". The concept is covered under the names above.
- The Kouider–Vestergaard survey "Connected factors in graphs – a survey" (Graphs and Combinatorics 21 (2005) 1–26, per search-result metadata) was not read. It may contain further multigraph/even-factor results.
- No "connected b-matching / connected b-factor in multigraphs" complexity paper beyond the above was found.

## Q3. Other literature on prescribed/bounded visit-count walks (k-walks, parity, 2-walks, robotics/arc routing)

### Takeaway
A small graph-theory literature surrounds the exact- and bounded-visit walk problems: k-walks, [a,b]-HAM, parity Hamiltonian cycles and 2-walks. The most recent and sharpest complexity work is Liu–Sheffield–Westover (2024), which gives tight P vs NP-hard dichotomies for bounded-degree and regular graphs, including the exact case a = b. No relevant robotics or arc-routing work on "exactly k visits per vertex" was found in this pass.

### Cited Findings
- Liu–Sheffield–Westover main theorems (closed walks): (Thm 1) in d-regular graphs, "[a,b]-HAM is NP-hard if either d is even and b < d/2, or d is odd and b < d. Otherwise, [a,b]-HAM ∈ P." (Thm 2) in max-degree-d graphs, "NP-hard if either a = 1 and b < d, or a > 1 and b/a < d − 1. Otherwise … ∈ P." (Thm 3) directed max-degree 3: "∈ P if a = b > 1, and NP-hard otherwise." (Thm 4) directed max-degree 4: "NP-hard for all b ≥ a ≥ 1." Easy direction for regular graphs (Lemma 2): "For even d, any d-regular graph is [d/2,d/2]-HAM. For odd d, any d-regular graph is [d,d]-HAM." Their abstract notes the known reductions produce graphs with "maximum degree growing linearly in b", and that they are, "to our knowledge … the first to consider the algorithmic complexity of [a,b]-HAM in general bounded degree graphs". — [arXiv:2405.16270](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16270)
- Jackson–Wormald give sufficient conditions for (exact) k-walks: toughness (Lemma 2.1(ii), from Broersma: an exact k-walk implies c(G−S) + (k−1)c₀(G−S) ≤ k|S|), k-trees (a k-tree gives a k-walk; a k-walk gives a (k+1)-tree), Theorem 5.6 ("If G is connected and δ(G) ≥ 2 then G² has an exact 2-walk"), Theorem 5.7 ("Every 3-connected planar graph has a 3-walk"), and Conjecture 2.1 (every 1/(k−1)-tough graph has a k-walk, k ≥ 2). — [Jackson & Wormald 1990](https://ajc-new.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/2/ajc-v2-p135.pdf)
- Related results cited by Liu et al.: Nishiyama et al., "The parity Hamiltonian cycle problem", Discrete Math. 341(3):606–626 (2018). A closed walk visiting every vertex an odd number of times is decidable in polynomial time, but the problem "becomes NP-hard when each edge can be traversed at most 3 times". Gao & Richter, "2-walks in circuit graphs", JCTB 62(2):259–267 (1994): every 3-connected planar graph is [1,2]-HAM. Schmid & Schmidt, "Computing 2-Walks in Polynomial Time", ACM TALG 14(2) (2018). Kouider & Vestergaard, "Even [a,b]-factors in graphs", Discuss. Math. Graph Theory 24(3):431–441 (2004): for a ≥ 2, any 2-connected graph with minimum degree an/(a+b) is [a,b]-HAM, per Liu et al.'s description. — [arXiv:2405.16270](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16270)
- MV-TSP application contexts (aircraft landing sequencing, scheduling job classes with sequence-dependent setups, Maximum Scatter TSP EPTAS, FPT Hamiltonian Cycle on bounded neighborhood diversity) are listed by Bérczi–Mnich–Vincze and Berger et al. — [EGRES TR-2020-19](https://egres.elte.hu/tr/egres-20-19.pdf); [arXiv:1804.06361](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06361)

### Inferences
- The exact-visit-count walk problem is well established in structural graph theory (k-traversable graphs / exact k-walks) and in TSP algorithmics (MV-TSP). The two communities rarely cite each other: Liu et al. do not cite MV-TSP, and the MV-TSP papers do not cite Broersma–Göbel or Jackson–Wormald. That probably explains why the result can look new.
- Liu et al.'s directed max-degree-3 result ("∈ P if a = b > 1") shows the exact case can behave differently from the at-most case in restricted classes.
